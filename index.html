The colosseum was carved from stone; seating surrounded a pit that was scarred with claw marks, dents, and chips. Faded stains from previous battles dotted the walls and bathed the floors. Each mark told the story of a battle won by one of the king's knights, of lives saved by their willingness to take on a beast alone.

It was a way to show the people that they were protected, and to remind them of how strong each individual knight was.

Gideon beamed as he tightened the straps on Wyatt's armor. The other man stood several inches taller than Gideon, with the dark eyes of a falcon and a crooked nose that spoke of years of combat. A scar stretched from below his left eye and curled under his chin. Under the plate armor and chainmail was his gambeson, a thick, quilted tunic that keeps the mail from pinching the knight's skin. His tunic was a rich purple color, brand new and freshly washed.

Wyatt gave Gideon a crooked grin. "Hey, don't worry about me. I'll be alright."

Gideon shrugged and pulled the mail hood over Wyatt's head. He carefully brushed his hair down so that it wouldn't catch. "I know. You always come out alright, but what if this time's different? What if this is the beast that gets you?"

Wyatt cupped Gideon's cheek. "It won't be, honey. I promise.

Gideon wheezed as his ribs bent under the arc of a training sword. His knee hit the ground and the knight who hit him snickered. "You don't belong here. Give up before someone finishes you off for good."

Gideon flipped him off and staggered over to the partition around the exposed hallways that circled the courtyard. His breathing was labored and he clutched his side; he knew a cloud of bruises was forming beneath his muted gray gambeson. The dye was uneven and the edges frayed. Gideon picked at the hem of his sleeve. His chainmail was old and rusted in places.

Knights moved across the courtyard with heavy feet; they swung weighty training swords and jeered with every successful hit. They shed their plate armor in favor of practicing in just chain mail and their thick wool gambesons. The gambesons were long and quilted with several layers of fabric, and lined with dyed leather. The fabric was dyed in bright reds, yellows, and blues save for one figure who wore purple. Fine embroidery woven with precious metals lined them and shimmered in the beating sunlight.

Squires darted around, dressed in tunics of the same colors. They offered their knights water and fresh weapons regularly, eager to prove their willingness to serve. Cloaked healers were lined up along the edge of the courtyard, heads bowed and hands folded as they waited to be called upon. Injuries weren't uncommon, but sorcerers were always on call to heal nobility.

Gideon searched for the figure in purple among the other nobles and smiled. Wyatt fought with the grace of a raptor in flight; he spun to dodge oncoming blows and wielded his sword as a falcon might wield his claws. Every movement was fluid and practiced and utterly natural. He had dark, short hair and a crooked nose, and his mouth was drawn into a thin frown.

Gideon offered Wyatt a cheeky smile and waved. The prince caught his eye and smiled back.

A wooden sword slammed into Wyatt's back, knocking him to the ground. All other sparring matches halted and a healer rushed forward with short, quick steps. Gideon stood and watched as Wyatt waved off the healer and struggled to his feet. He staggered to Gideon and sat beside him with a heavy sigh. "That wasn't embarrassing," Wyatt said.

"What do you mean? I didn't see anything," Gideon remarked with a flutter of his eyelashes and a wide smile. "Unless you're referring to how you were distracted by my presence and got pummeled by Darion."

Wyatt rolled his eyes and nudged Gideon's shoulder with his own. "I don't want to hear it from you. I missed you yesterday, you weren't at dinner."

"I was preoccupied," Gideon murmured. He looked away and focused on the other knights. They resumed training. He plastered on a smile and winked at Wyatt. "It sounds like you missed me."

"I did," Wyatt agreed. He scooted closer to Gideon. "I had to have dinner with my parents."

"Nicole wasn't there?"

"No, our future queen was too busy for family dinner. Not that I blame her, of course."

"It's understandable," Gideon agreed. He leaned against Wyatt. "I wouldn't want to dine with my annoying little brother, either."

Wyatt laughed and cuffed Gideon on the ear. "Rude! You can't speak to me like that, I'm your prince."

"My deepest apologies, your highness. I wasn't aware that royal status exempts you from being a complete ass."

Wyatt barked out a laugh and left a gentle kiss on Gideon's cheek. "I need to get back to practice. Are you still having fun?"

"Of course," Gideon hummed. He forced a small smile. "I always enjoy myself when I'm near you."

Gideon watched him leave and let his gaze linger on his gambeson. The rich purple swallowed the light around it, and golden thread traced glittering images of Wyatt's spirit. Feathers spread from his shoulders, two boys danced to the music of a lone harpist beneath them, and a knight fought a manticore across the front.

He wondered what images might rise from his own gambeson if he could afford the service. Very few people specify what they want woven into their clothes; instead, they hire a mystic to read their soul and embroider what they see. It's not uncommon for nobles to flounce around in silk cloaks with gold and silver images lining the edges or filling out the entire length of fabric.

Gideon heaved himself to his feet and took a deep breath before wiggling back into his chainmail. A hand clamped on his shoulder, and he flinched before turning around.

Sir Richard loomed over him with narrowed eyes and thin lips. His skin was weathered with time and a scar stretched under his eye and curled beneath his jaw. A sneer tugged on his lip and he loomed over Gideon. "What makes you think that you can take a break?"

Gideon deflated. "I'm injured, sir."

"Clearly not enough to warrant a break if you have the energy to seduce the prince." He scoffed and turned to look at the knights. "Maybe you need to spend some time somewhere else to appreciate the opportunity the prince has given you. Instead of reporting here tomorrow, you'll report to the library. Our librarian will have something for you to do."

That order left Gideon with a knot in his stomach.

He had no idea where the library was.

-

The next morning Gideon wandered the halls in search of the library. He finally caved and asked a servant. She was a young girl with a basket of laundry on her hip and a deadpan expression. "Why would you go to the library?"

"I'm being punished."

"For what?"

"Being poor. Can you please point me in the right direction?"

She smiled and rattled off a short set of directions. "You know, they say a monster lives in the library. That's why nobody ever goes there."

"I thought it was because they couldn't read."

She shrugged and sauntered off.

The library was hidden behind two large oak doors. Gideon knocked. Nobody answered him, so he peeked his head inside and gasped. Bookshelves reached for the high, cavernous ceilings, and each one was crammed with old tomes. Shelves lined the walls, and they were placed so that the room felt like a fortress. Stacks of books were piled around like roadblocks, and the only sources of light were the candles that was balanced precariously on edges of shelves and on the unsteady stacks.

"What can I do for you, sir?"

Gideon jumped as the low, croaky voice. He turned and saw an ancient, wizened man with one wide eye and one squinty eye, and stringy white hair that fell over his shoulders like a threadbare tapestry. One of his sleeves was tied off at the elbow. He was dressed in regal robes and wore a kind, airy smile.

Gideon marched forward and stood at parade rest. "Hello sir. Are you the librarian?"

"Yes, my name's Peter. Do you need something?"

"Yes, Sir Richard sent me as a form of punishment. I'm at your disposal."

Peter hummed. "Yes, that's right. He visited me yesterday and informed me that you would be studying monsters." He reached under the counter and pulled out a large novel. "This is our bestiary. It's been around since the beginning of our current dynasty. When King Leon took the throne, he had all of our knowledge compiled into this tome and it's now the standard text for the hunting and killing of monsters."

Gideon rolled his eyes. "Great, so I'll be reading while I serve my time."

"Yes, you were sent to the library. What did you think you would be doing?"

"Honestly, I hadn't really thought about it. I assumed I would be cleaning or something. Somehow, this is worse."

Peter shrugged. "I suggest you get to reading. It's fairly long."

Gideon groaned, picked up the book and traced the large golden seal that was stamped onto the cover. It was shaped like a manticore who was being stabbed in the chest by a knight. He wandered around and found that the library was larger than he thought, and all of the shelves were placed at awkward angles, making it difficult to navigate. There were times that he heard soft, creeping, jangling footsteps around him, or he saw a small flame moving where the others stood still. After several minutes he found a small table and set the book down and snatched a candle from a nearby shelf. It barely gave off enough light to read by. He plopped down, opened to the first page, and started reading. The words were written in an ornate, blocky script that must have taken hours to perfect. He took in the table of contents and yawned. A little nap before he started couldn't hurt, right?

-

Gideon awoke to the smell of rotting eggs. He jumped when he turned his head and saw someone towering over him. It was a girl around his age with an old, dark wood pipe dangling from her lips. Thick yellow smoke curled from the bowl like a heavy fog. She wore a plain black dress with a matching cloak, and a pair of boots with far too many buckles on them. A long, pale scar stretched across her throat like a crescent moon.

Her amber eyes were molten in the glow of her pipe. "Gideon," she greeted him. Her voice was soft, but there was a faint rattle in the back of her throat.

"How do you know my name?"

"Peter told me. He asked me to check on you, see if you've made any progress." Her eyes zeroed in on the drool spot he left on one of the pages, then to the knife at his side. "You're not allowed to have weapons in here."

"I'm sorry, who are you?"

"I'm Cyra, Peter's apprentice. I'm going to have to ask you to surrender your weapons."

"Peter didn't say anything about surrendering my weapons," Gideon whined. "Why is that a rule? I wouldn't just attack someone without prompting."

Cyra raised an eyebrow. "Libraries are sacred places. Everyone inside deserves to feel safe."

"Whatever." Gideon slid the sheath off of his belt and handed it over.

Cyra glanced at his boot. "Forgetting something?"

"Oh, sorry. I always forget about that one." Gideon pulled another knife from his boot and handed it over. "Will I get those back when I leave?"

Cyra shrugged and disappeared into the shadows.

Gideon hunched over the book and started reading. The book was organized by creature, and each creature had at least ten pages dedicated to it. The first passage was long and lyrical, and it came with several detailed illustrations.

Gideon's eyes traced the dark lines of the ogre figures. The violence was graphic, and the blood was colored in with bright red ink. The children depicted were screaming and crying and many of them were being gnawed on by ogres.

He read over his notes.

"Ogres are tall, bipedal  creatures with disfigured heads and mouths full of crooked, rotting teeth. They reside in the mountain regions. They have small, cloudy eyes, and are very strong. Ogres have low intelligence and an insatiable bloodlust. They're known for killing families and stealing children to eat back in their dens."

Well that's disgusting. Gideon rolled up his parchment and tucked it into his belt. That was enough for one day. He left the book on the table and open before heading out.

There was a faint jangling, and Cyra's voice cut through the dark. "Forgetting something?"

Gideon jumped. "Why did you sneak up on me?"

Cyra didn't respond. Instead, she pointed to the front desk where his knives sat. "How did your reading go?"

"I didn't learn anything new. Most of this is common knowledge."

Cyra hummed. "I wonder if you wouldn't benefit from reading another book." She slipped her hand into her cloak and pulled out an old, battered book. It was bound in simple leather and had no markings. "This isn't approved by the kingdom, there's no seal."

Cyra put a finger over her lips and a ghost of a smile flickered over our mouth. "Our little secret."

Gideon tucked the book under his arm and shrugged. "Fine, whatever you think. You're probably a book expert. I'll see you tomorrow, Cyra."

She waved him off, and Gideon marched to his rooms.

-

Gideon returned to his room, and felt heavy in his pocket. He lit a candle, took the book out, and flipped it open.

The title page was written in tall, skinny cursive.

Folktales Collected by P & R Eccles

"Folktales? Really? Does she think I'm a child?" Gideon muttered to himself and sighed before turning to the next page. There were no dedications and there was no index, just the title of the first story. "The Ogre and his Children."

Gideon's lip curled. He knew this story. His mother told it to him when he wouldn't eat, or when he didn't want to bathe. He wasn't in the mood to read another bloody story where children die and get eaten by a monster for not being obedient.

He went to close the book, but the candles flickered and flared. Their white light focused on the book, so Gideon started to read.

"There once was an Ogre named Cedric. He lived in the mountains all alone because he thought that his smile might scare others. Sometimes he would get visitors who needed help, and he was always glad to lend a hand.

"One of these visitors was a brutal man with two children. He beat them and neglected them, so Cedric took the children to live with him. Their father came to look for them and threatened to hurt them, so Cedric protected them. Their father ran to the nearest village and told them that he was attacked.

"The villagers created a mob and killed Cedric, and the children were forced back into their father's custody."

That wasn't the story that Gideon had been told. He remembered the villagers killing an unnamed ogre because he'd stolen a man's children.

The change prodded at his mind as he laid down to go to sleep.

Gideon returned to the library the next morning. He found his table just as he had left it; bestiary open and candle lit. There were two more candles, and together they gave off enough light to read comfortably. Gideon shook his head and opened the bestiary. The next section was over pixies, and it was several pages longer than the ogre section.

When he finished reading, he reviewed his notes.

"Pixies are one of the last species of Fae left in our land. According to the bestiary, they are manipulative, cunning, and very charming. They radiate energy and light to disorient their foes, and move quickly to evade capture. They create music to lure their enemies to them and trap them in dances that last as long as the pixies play it. Never accept food from them, and never accumulate debt."

Gideon rolled his notes up and felt the Eccles book brush against him in his cloak. He took it out and flipped it open to the story called "Dance with the Pixies."

Stories like this one had been passed around to discourage kids from sneaking out at night and from indulging in anything other than chamber music.

"In a village near the border between the Faerie Realm and our own, pixies were a common sight. They started by attending dances and providing music, and then visited more frequently to give the villagers blessings and charms to help their crops.

"A group of knights passed through one day and commented on their fruitful harvest. They demanded that the villagers hand over their crop, and when they refused, the knights burned the village down. Black smoke rose like life's breath from the ashen buildings.

"The pixies came to visit as this happened and they enchanted the knights to dance until they died. The pixies looked for survivors, and upon finding none, they enchanted the ruins so that lush plants would always grow there, and they could never be uprooted or burned down."

Gideon closed the book and sat back in his seat. That wasn't right; the story he was told growing up said that the pixies had the villagers under a spell, and that they starved and danced themselves into exhaustion before dropping dead where they stood. The knights were supposed to try and save them, not kill them.

"You didn't leave your weapons at the door."

"Sorry, I forgot." Gideon offered Cyra a tight smile. "I've been reading that book you gave me."

"Oh yeah? What do you think of it?"

"I'm still thinking. Do you want my knives?"

"Yeah."

Gideon passed them over and Cyra disappeared. He waited for her to return, but the heavy steps that approached weren't hers. Gideon beamed when he saw Wyatt. He shot out of his seat and leapt into Wyatt's arms. "Wyatt! I've missed you!"

"And I you, Gideon." He caressed Gideon's cheek and smiled. "I've been trying to convince Richard to let you back into training, but he won't be moved. He's convinced that you need to do some dull work before you can really appreciate a knight's training."

"I'm not surprised, Richard's always been pretty thick." Gideon giggled and leaned into the hug.

"What have you been up to?" Wyatt asked. He looked at the bestiary and scrunched up his nose. "Ugh. I've spent my fair share of time with that. It's so long."

"Yeah, I haven't been enjoying it."

"What about this other book? I don't recognize it."

"Oh that's… just something the librarian recommended. For my down time." Gideon tucked the Eccles book into his cloak with a smile. He hesitated before asking, "do you remember the folktale 'Dance with the Pixies?'"

"Yeah, it was one of my favorites as a kid! I never wanted to leave the castle at night, which was for the best. Danger is everywhere, Gideon, that's why we train to protect people who can't protect themselves. Why do you ask?"

Gideon hummed and nodded. "Just wondering."

They chatted until Wyatt had to return to training.

The faint glow of Cyra's pipe appeared next to him, and Gideon jumped. "Why? Cyra, why do you keep doing that?"

"I think it's funny, Gideon. Is the prince your boyfriend?" The scar that smiled across her throat cast a dark shadow down her neck.

"Yes he is."

"He's cute. Big arms."

"He's very cute," Gideon confirmed. A blush crept up his neck and he averted his eyes. "And yeah, all he does is train."

"Good for you," Cyra grinned. "Just… be careful."

Gideon watched her walk away and packed his things to leave.

-

Gideon arrived at the library early the next day. He dropped the knife on his hip on the front desk before beelining to his table and opening the bestiary. Today's lesson was over dragons. The knights always spoke of finding the last of the dragons and slaying them, but no one ever expected it to actually happen. Sir Richard was a member of the party that killed the last known dragon, but the kingdom was too paranoid to stop teaching about the creatures.

Gideon's notes were brief.

"Dragons are greedy, and are particularly territorial over their hoards. They hoard treasure that they've stolen, and kill anyone that tries to get close. Reclaiming your treasure from these beasts is very difficult due to their aggressive nature. They should be killed on sight to prevent further risk to the kingdom. The only way to ensure their death is decapitation."

Gideon closed the bestiary and opened the Eccles book. He found the story called "The Knight Who Slayed the Golden Dragon."

Gideon took a deep breath and began reading.

"The King and his knights heard tales of a dragon whose scales shimmered like gold and whose eyes were molten in the sunlight. They told the King of her riches and how she collected precious metals and gems by the ton.

"The King led a party to find the dragon. It didn't take long for them to ambush her and push her into a corner. The dragon fought them by breathing golden fire and trying to poison them with her yellow smoke, but the King's best knight was able to cut her head off so that they could take her gold back to the kingdom."

Gideon frowned. Where was the lie? Where was the hidden change he had learned to look for? That's exactly how his mother told it when he was young. She told it to teach him not to be greedy; you don't want to be like the dragon, who hoarded her wealth and killed trespassers. Then again… wasn't it the King who was being greedy? He came in and killed the dragon, who did nothing to provoke him.

Heavy footsteps approached, and Gideon turned around with a wide smile. "Wyatt-"

"Stand up to greet your superiors." Richard's voice was a low growl.

Gideon shot to his feet and stood at attention. "Sorry, sir."

"I didn't give you permission to speak." Richard stalked around the table and put his hand out. "Give me your notes. Peter's been sending me reports, but they looked a little too good. I need to make sure he's not going easy on you."

Gideon gave him his scroll and felt the warm discomfort of anxiety flood his system. Richard scanned his writing and scoffed. "This is mediocre at best. What have you been doing here if you're not studying the bestiary."

"Learning," Gideon said quietly.

"Learning what? You're supposed to be learning how to kill these monsters. You can't leave any of them alive; you don't want to spend your life looking over your shoulder."

Gideon looked over Richard's shoulder and saw Cyra standing in the shadows. Her pipe was in her hand, and smoke leaked from her mouth and pooled on the floor.

Richard froze and turned around. "Who's there? I smell-oh, God no."

Cyra melted into the shadows and reappeared from behind a different shelf. Richard tried to lunge at her, but she side-stepped him and he fell over a pile of books. She took a knife from her belt–Gideon's knife–and slashed at him. Her blade sliced through the front of his tunic and left a long, seeping line across his chest.

Richard cried out and jabbed at her with his sword. She blocked his first swing, but he disarmed her and knocked her to the ground. Her pipe clattered across the floor. "You… I remember you. How could I forget? You got away."

"No," Cyra growled, "you couldn't kill me. You're the one who didn't finish the job."

Richard pressed his blade into her cheek and turned her head. "Your scar is beautiful. I'll make sure you don't come back this time." He raised his sword in preparation to swing.

Gideon's mind raced and tried to decide what to do. On one hand, helping Richard kill Cyra would earn him immediate respect. There would be no question about whether or not Gideon belonged with the knights.

On the other hand, this was Cyra. She was tough and scary, but she was also kind. Kind enough to speak civilly with him, and kind enough to open his eyes to a truth he never would have known about otherwise.

Gideon reached down and unsheathed the knife from his boot. He kicked Richard before he could bring his sword down and tossed Cyra his knife. Cyra took it and sliced the back of Richard's ankle. His achille's tendon made a wooden cracking sound, and he collapsed to the ground in a writhing heap.

Gideon offered Cyra a hand up, and she took it. "I'm sorry you've gotten caught up in this mess. I can't imagine it's easy for you."

"No, but it's not easy for you, either." Gideon glanced at Richard. "Are you going to let him live?"

"No," she said, testing the blade's weight. "I can't spend my life looking over my shoulder, waiting for him to appear." She gripped Richard's hair in one hand and plunged the knife into his throat with the other. She dragged the blade across his throat so that his wound matched hers. "There, now I know he won't come back."

Gideon staggered, lightheaded from the sudden gore. "What will you do now?"

Cyra shrugged. "Whatever I want. The kingdom thinks I'm dead, and I've killed the man who tried to kill me. I have nothing left to fear. Right?" Her gaze was pointed, and a smile tugged on her lips. "You have an obligation to the prince, but…"

Gideon nodded. "I think my obligation goes a little bit deeper than actions. I want to help him understand, Cyra. The kingdom will be a better place for it."

Cyra nodded and melted into the shadows. "Thank you, Gideon."

Gideon watched her disappear and walked over to pick up her pipe. The bowl was clean, but the button and bit were charred and had sharp teeth marks in them. The smell of sulfur lingered, but the source was gone for good.
