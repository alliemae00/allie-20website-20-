Absinthe or Abstinence: An Alcoholic's Guide to Discovering Sobriety

Storm stared the sirens down before bringing the microphone to her mouth. The opening notes of Untitled (How Could This Happen To Me?) by Simple Plan crackled out of Hector's shitty karaoke machine and she shot Doc one last scathing look before she started to sing.

After some bad weather beached her ship on a skerry, Storm woke up to find her boat was still seaworthy, but there were thin scratch marks on the hull and a harem of sirens whispering to one another. When they spotted her they started singing in an attempt to draw her off of the ship, but Storm was so drunk that her response was to start singing back. This resulted in both sides deciding to strike a deal; they would have a karaoke battle to determine if Storm and her passengers got to live.

After six hours of no drinking, Storm hasn't been this sober in over two years. Doc had confiscated her last bottle of whiskey and held it just out of reach, and her head throbbed enough to discourage any attempts to get it back. The rest of Storm's stash was scattered across the skerry her ship was beached on. And yeah, getting the sirens drunk was a good call on Doc's part, but Storm wasn't sure why she wasn't allowed to have any more alcohol.

Doc was the little fucker who hired her for this expedition. She was 6 '2 and radiated raw academia; her qualifications included always smelling like stale soy sauce, wearing that damn lab coat, and apparently she had a PhD in marine biology. She found Storm and pitched the easiest job in the world. All she had to do was take Doc's little party of grad students to the Bering Sea to find some sea lions and save the ocean or something to that effect. Storm didn't agree until the pay was doubled.

Storm finished singing, and the grad students stared at her with wide eyes and open mouths. Doc sighed, buried her face in her hands, and tossed Storm the bottle. She caught it and popped the cork before taking a long drink.

Storm frowned at their reactions. What the fuck was that supposed to mean? She glanced down at the sirens to gauge their reactions. They had the same wide eyes and open mouths as the others. She looked down the mouth of her bottle and turned it this way and that so the sun's light turned the dark liquid into honey.

Doc sidled up to her and offered her a tight smile. "Good job, Cap. I think you've got em on the ropes."

Storm scrunched up her nose. Soy sauce. "Thanks Doc."

The sirens flipped through their song selection and started it. For some reason they got the better karaoke machine; the first notes of Sabrina Carpenter's Espresso filtered out as smooth as heavy cream.

"These trendy motherfuckers would. Whatever happened to the classics?" Storm rolled her eyes. "Who do these sirens think they are? They're supposed to be fish ladies."

"We're just getting old, Cap. Anyway, sirens are traditionally bird women, not fish."

"Whatever. I wasn't expecting fucking seals."

"...they're sea lions."

Storm considered whether the grad students would rat her out if she pushed their supervisor into the harem of sirens. If they revolted she would push the rest of them in, too. Maybe that would be enough to satiate the sirens' bloodlust.

Would she still get paid if that happened?

She shook her head and focused on the performance. The sirens what was intended to be sexy choreography to go with the song, and she wondered where they learned it from. It was muddled by whiskey, making it sloppy and uncoordinated. There's no way Youtube had infected the sirens way out here. Fuck garbage pollution, if creatures of myth were singing about coffee then there were larger problems to worry about.

Storm said as much.

Doc nodded in agreement, but one of the grad students, Jennifer, threw her a scandalized look. "I'll have you know that this song is actually super appropriate for them to sing."

The leader of the sirens belted out the first note, and Storm grinned. The siren's singing reminded her of a child that was a little too ambitious with how much mashed potato he could fit in his mouth. They spilled out in clumps and piled up incoherently.

Even if the song was appropriate for their situation, Storm would never know because not a single word of it reached her ears. "Do you think I'm going to win?"

Doc and Jennifer looked at each other and frowned. "It'll be a close thing."

"Fuck you guys." Storm grumbled and took another drink of whiskey. "I was thinking about sharing this, but if you're just gonna be mean then I won't."

"I think you sounded great." Jennifer grinned when Storm passed her the bottle.

"Do you think they'll hold up their end of the bargain?" Doc asked.

Storm considered this. "I wouldn't." Her eyes traced the claw marks that lined the hull of her ship. The sirens couldn't scale the ship thanks to the thorough waxing she had gotten done, but that also meant that they were trapped.

The sun sank below the horizon like an ambitious hamster rolling its ball off of a table. Pale blue turned to the color of the orange juice stain on Storm's white t-shirt, and then bled into the dark purple of the bruise she got from running into a door earlier.

Something that all of the old sailors told her was that they missed the silence of being on the open water. They miss the caress of the waves as they rock the ship, like a parent lulling their child to sleep. The sea is spoken of like a soul that's been intertwined with theirs, and being apart is the hardest thing they could do.

Storm thought the ocean was a bitch. She had an obligation to the sea; it was the source of her income, but it was also what isolated her and what brought her and her passengers to this island.

Storm took a deep breath and snatched the rest of her whiskey from Jennifer. It was still a few fingers full, so she took a drink and marched into the kitchen to find a rag. She shoved it into the bottle as far as it would go and snatched her lighter from the drawer before heading back outside. Doc and Jennifer shot her a worried glance before she braced herself against the rails and jumped overboard.

Her leg snapped and the broken bones shifted when she hit the ground; raw nerve endings grinded against each other and she shrieked in pain. The sirens all turned to look at her and squinted. They tried to process what just happened through the whiskey fog, and after a minute they did. One of them called for a charge, and they slid across the rocks towards their next meal.

Storm lit the end of the rag and held it in the air. The sirens stared at it with awestruck gazes before Storm threw it between them and herself. "Peace out, bitches."

The bottle exploded, killing the sirens closest to it and propelling the ship off of the rocks. It rocked sharply before righting itself and drifting aimlessly.

Storm's body burned alongside the sirens, but at least her passengers were safe.
